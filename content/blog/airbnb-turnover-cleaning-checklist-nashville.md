If you host on Airbnb or VRBO in Nashville, you already know the review that hurts the most: *"the place wasn't clean."* Cleanliness is the one category guests judge without mercy — and in a market as competitive as Nashville, where thousands of short-term rentals fight for the same weekend visitors, a single missed detail can cost you a five-star streak.

A turnover clean is not a regular house clean. It has a deadline (your next check-in), a standard (hotel-level presentation), and a hidden job (spotting damage and restocking before the next guest walks in). Here is the checklist a professional turnover should cover, room by room.

## Living areas and bedrooms

The first impression happens in the first ten seconds, so presentation matters as much as sanitation.

- Strip all beds and remake them with fresh linens — every stay, no exceptions
- Fluff and arrange pillows and throws the same way every time (photos help keep it consistent)
- Dust all reachable surfaces: nightstands, dressers, headboards, TV consoles, lamps
- Check under beds and sofas for anything guests left behind
- Vacuum carpets and rugs, then mop hard floors
- Wipe mirrors, switch plates, remotes and door handles — the high-touch spots guests never see cleaned but always notice when they're not
- Empty every trash can and replace liners

**Host tip:** keep two or three full linen sets per bed. Laundry is the biggest time-eater in a turnover, and spare sets let the cleaning move forward while sheets are still in the wash.

## Kitchen

Guests forgive a small kitchen; they never forgive a dirty one.

- Wash, dry and put away any dishes left behind
- Clean counters, backsplash and sink until they shine
- Wipe the exterior of all appliances — and the *inside* of the microwave, which guests always check
- Check the fridge shelf by shelf: remove leftovers, wipe spills
- Run and empty the dishwasher
- Restock basics: coffee, filters, dish soap, sponge, paper towels, trash bags
- Sweep and mop the floor, including under small appliances

## Bathrooms

This is where five-star reviews are won or lost.

- Scrub and sanitize the toilet — bowl, seat, hinges and base
- Clean the shower and tub: walls, glass, fixtures, drain hair (yes, every time)
- Polish mirrors, faucets and chrome until spotless
- Wipe the vanity and check the drawers for personal items left behind
- Replace all towels with fresh sets, folded consistently
- Restock toilet paper (at least two rolls visible), shampoo, conditioner and hand soap
- Empty the trash and mop the floor, corners included

## The walkthrough: the step most hosts skip

A professional turnover ends with a final inspection pass, because the cleaner is your eyes on the property:

- Test lights, AC settings and the door lock or keypad
- Look for damage, stains or missing items — and photograph anything unusual
- Confirm staging matches your listing photos
- Check that supplies left for guests are visible and complete
- Set the thermostat and lighting for arrival

That damage report matters more than most hosts realize: to claim through Airbnb's resolution process, you need evidence collected *before* the next guest checks in. A cleaner who photographs and reports issues the same day protects your income, not just your reviews.

## Timing: the Nashville checkout window

Most Nashville listings run checkout at 10–11 AM and check-in at 3–4 PM. That gives a 4–6 hour window to do everything above — tighter if you host a full house near Broadway or a multi-bedroom place in East Nashville that sleeps ten bachelorette guests.

A realistic budget for a thorough turnover:

- **1-bedroom condo:** 1.5–2.5 hours
- **2–3 bedroom home:** 2.5–4 hours
- **Large group house:** 4+ hours, often with two cleaners

If your current turnovers are "done" in 45 minutes, something on this checklist is not happening.

## Common mistakes that show up in reviews

- **Reusing linens that "look clean."** Guests can tell. Wash everything, every stay.
- **Ignoring smells.** A space can be spotless and still smell like the last guest's cooking. Air out, take trash all the way out, and never mask odors with heavy sprays — many guests are sensitive to fragrance.
- **Inconsistent staging.** When the sofa cushions, mugs and towels are arranged differently every stay, the listing feels amateur. Consistency reads as professionalism.
- **No supply tracking.** Running out of toilet paper mid-stay is a guaranteed complaint. Your cleaner should flag low stock before it becomes a 2 AM guest message.

## DIY or hire a pro?

Plenty of Nashville hosts start by cleaning their own units — and with one listing and a flexible schedule, that can work. The math changes when you add a second property, live outside the city, or start getting back-to-back bookings. One delayed flight or sick day, and you're scrubbing a tub at 2 PM with a guest texting "can we check in early?"

A dedicated turnover team gives you a repeatable standard, a same-day damage report, and your weekends back. For hosts running multiple units, it's usually the difference between a side hustle and a business.
